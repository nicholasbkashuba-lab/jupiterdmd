Signs you may need a filling

Early decay often has no symptoms at all, which is why it is usually found at a check-up or on an X-ray. As it grows you may notice sensitivity to sweets, hot or cold, a rough edge your tongue keeps finding, food catching between two teeth, or a dark spot. An old filling that has cracked, leaks or has decay around its edge also needs attention.

What happens at the appointment

The tooth is numbed, the decayed part is removed and the space is cleaned. The filling material is then placed, shaped to fit your bite and polished. Most fillings are done in a single visit. Your lip and tongue may stay numb for a couple of hours, so take care eating until the feeling returns.

Choosing a filling material

Fillings can be made from several materials, including tooth-colored composite resin and silver amalgam. Each has trade-offs in strength, appearance and cost. Dr. Forte will explain which suits the size and position of your cavity, and answer questions about any material, including the safety of amalgam, before you decide.

FAQ

Questions patients ask

Does getting a filling hurt?

The tooth is numbed before any work begins, so most people feel pressure rather than pain. Some sensitivity for a few days afterwards is common and usually settles on its own. Call the office if it doesn't.

Can old silver fillings be replaced?

Yes. Old fillings that are worn, cracked or leaking can be replaced. Dr. Forte will tell you whether a filling actually needs replacing or is still doing its job.

How long does a filling take?

Most fillings are finished in one appointment. Larger or multiple fillings take longer, and the office will tell you how much time to set aside when you book.
